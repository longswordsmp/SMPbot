# Fifty Game Ideas
### Filtered through the September 2026 market, not the 2025 one

---

## The filter every idea had to pass

These are not fifty noun-swaps. Each one had to survive five tests drawn from the research in `00-STRATEGY-BRIEF.md`:

1. **Legible in under 30 seconds.** The title states the loop. You understand it before you finish reading the tile.
2. **It has a Day 8-28 answer.** The June 2026 ranker scores retention in Day 1, Day 2-7 and Day 8-28 buckets. Every idea below names the system that brings someone back on day twenty.
3. **Co-play is designed in.** "Intentional co-play" is a headline discovery signal and almost nobody builds for it deliberately.
4. **The intellectual property is ownable.** No borrowed memes. The Mementum lawsuit made that a live liability.
5. **No banned or at-risk mechanics.** No media feeds, no watch-to-earn, no hidden-odds crates, no design that assumes under-13 whales.

Build cost is marked **Cheap** for a two-person eight-week build, **Medium** for twelve weeks, **Heavy** for a real team.

---

## If you only build one

My ranked shortlist from the fifty, on the balance of upside against build cost:

| | Idea | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **#6 Skytree** | One shared tree, eight players, pure co-op idle. The strongest co-play signal on the list and genuinely cheap to build |
| 2 | **#1 Snatch a Stomper** | The full design already exists in `01-GAME-DESIGN.md` |
| 3 | **#11 Lighthouse** | The *Animal Hospital* lane won every award at RDC 2026 and has exactly one dominant title |
| 4 | **#21 Lawn Order** | Tower defense hybrid; *Plants vs Brainrots* proved the lane at 8.65M concurrent |
| 5 | **#26 Cryptid Cards** | Trading economies are the genre's long tail, and this is a trading economy with a game attached |

## Lane 1 — Raid & Rivalry
*The formula loop, rebuilt so the other players are teammates.*

**1. Snatch a Stomper**
Loop: Two crews of four. Ordinary objects with legs earn money on your warehouse floor, and the rival crew can carry them out the door.
Money: Multipliers, auto-collect, crew VIP, server-wide luck boosts.
Day 8-28: Persistent crews, weekly league, seasons, trading.
Build: Cheap. Full design already written.

**2. Repo Crew**
Loop: You are repossession agents. Hook a rival crew's vehicle with a tow truck and drag it back to your impound lot before they unhook it.
Money: Better trucks, faster winches, impound slots, cosmetic liveries.
Day 8-28: A vehicle collection with visible holes, plus crew impound rankings.
Build: Medium. Vehicle physics is the risk; get it feeling good or the whole thing collapses.

**3. Hive Heist**
Loop: Bug crews raid each other's honey vaults. You are small, the world is enormous, and honey is heavy.
Money: Carry capacity, vault locks, bug species, wing upgrades.
Day 8-28: Species collection, hive levels, seasonal flower blooms.
Build: Cheap. Insects are cheap to model and instantly plushable.

**4. Moving Day**
Loop: Your house is full of furniture. A rival crew is moving it out the front door while you move it back in. Nobody wins, everybody laughs.
Money: Dollies, straps, bigger vans, furniture sets.
Day 8-28: Furniture collection, house upgrades, decorating scores.
Build: Cheap. The comedy carries it. Very strong clip potential.

**5. Pocket Pirates**
Loop: Tiny pirate crews on a kitchen table. Sail a shoebox galleon, raid the sugar bowl, defend your treasure from the crew at the other end.
Money: Ships, crew cosmetics, cannons, treasure slots.
Day 8-28: Ship progression, treasure collection, crew fleets.
Build: Medium. The scale gag is the whole appeal and it needs careful art direction.

## Lane 2 — Grow & Tend
*The idle loop, made social.*

**6. Skytree**
Loop: One enormous tree per server. All eight players tend it together. It grows while everyone is offline, and what it becomes depends on what the whole server fed it.
Money: Seeds, fertilisers, branch plots, weather calls.
Day 8-28: The tree persists across sessions for your grove. Seasonal tree types. Grove rankings.
Build: Cheap. **This is the single strongest co-play design on the list** because cooperation is not encouraged, it is the only mode.

**7. Grow a Goo**
Loop: Cultivate slime. It splits, it mutates, it absorbs what you feed it, and it never stops jiggling.
Money: Feed types, splitting accelerators, containment tanks, mutation rerolls with disclosed odds.
Day 8-28: A mutation index with hundreds of entries, cross-breeding with other players' goo.
Build: Cheap. Slime is cheap to render and enormously satisfying to watch.

**8. Reef**
Loop: Grow a coral garden. Different corals attract different fish. The fish are the collection, the coral is the work.
Money: Coral species, water quality upgrades, tank space, rare fish lures.
Day 8-28: A fish index, seasonal migrations, visiting other players' reefs.
Build: Medium. Beautiful, calm, and tonally unlike anything in the genre, which is an advantage.

**9. Spore**
Loop: Grow mushrooms. Your spores drift onto neighbouring plots and cross-breed with theirs, which means your neighbour's choices change what you can grow.
Money: Spore types, drift control, plot expansion, humidity gear.
Day 8-28: Cross-breeding means the interesting species only exist through other people. Genuine interdependence.
Build: Cheap. The drift mechanic is the whole idea and it is mostly maths.

**10. Compost Kings**
Loop: You grow by rotting things. Throw trash in the pile, wait, and harvest what crawls out.
Money: Pile expansions, accelerants, creature slots, rare trash.
Day 8-28: A creature index, pile levels, seasonal rot events.
Build: Cheap. Gross-out humour is reliably popular with the core demographic.

## Lane 3 — Co-op Shift
*The Animal Hospital lane. It swept every award at RDC 2026 and has one dominant title.*

**11. Lighthouse**
Loop: Two to four keepers run a lighthouse through the night. Log what you see in the fog. Some of what you see should not be logged.
Money: Lamp upgrades, cosmetics, equipment, extra shifts.
Day 8-28: A logbook that persists, anomaly encyclopaedia, keeper ranks, escalating nights.
Build: Medium. One building, heavy atmosphere, low asset count. **The best cost-to-impact ratio in this lane.**

**12. Deep Freeze**
Loop: An Antarctic research station. Run the daily tasks, keep the generator alive, and work out which of your readings are wrong.
Money: Gear, station upgrades, cosmetics, expedition passes.
Day 8-28: Station progression across sessions, research tree, seasonal expeditions.
Build: Medium. Cold, isolation and a short asset list.

**13. Museum After Dark**
Loop: Night guards cataloguing exhibits. The exhibits move when nobody is looking, and the catalogue has to be right by morning.
Money: Torches, cameras, guard cosmetics, wing unlocks.
Day 8-28: A persistent catalogue, new wings, exhibit lore that unfolds weekly.
Build: Medium. Endlessly extensible by adding exhibits, which is cheap weekly content.

**14. Last Ferry**
Loop: Run the night ferry. Check tickets, manage the crossing, and decide what to do about the passenger who is not on the manifest.
Money: Ferry upgrades, uniforms, route unlocks.
Day 8-28: Route progression, passenger encyclopaedia, weekly crossings.
Build: Cheap. One boat, one dock, one stretch of water.

**15. Night Kitchen**
Loop: A co-op restaurant on the late shift. The orders start normal. They do not stay normal.
Money: Kitchen upgrades, recipes, cosmetics, station unlocks.
Day 8-28: Recipe collection, restaurant levels, weekly menus.
Build: Medium. Co-op cooking is proven fun and produces excellent clips.

## Lane 4 — The Clock
*Fixed-timer survival. 99 Nights in the Forest proved the shape and won Best Survival at RDC 2026.*

**16. 30 Days at Sea**
Loop: A raft, four players, thirty days. Fish, purify water, expand the raft, survive the storms.
Money: Raft parts, tools, cosmetics, starting supplies.
Day 8-28: Meta-progression between runs, raft blueprints, weekly storm seeds.
Build: Medium. Water is the technical risk; everything else is cheap.

**17. Tide**
Loop: Your island floods on a fixed schedule. Build upward before it does. Everything below the waterline is gone.
Money: Building materials, pumps, cosmetics, foundation slots.
Day 8-28: A persistent island that carries across sessions, blueprint collection.
Build: Cheap. The rising water is one number and it creates all the tension.

**18. The Long Drive**
Loop: One car, four players, a fuel gauge and a very long road. Scavenge, repair, keep going.
Money: Car parts, cosmetic liveries, starting fuel, radio stations.
Day 8-28: Garage progression, route map, weekly road events.
Build: Medium. Procedural road is the trick and it is well-trodden.

**19. Winter Count**
Loop: Survive a set number of nights. Gather by day, defend by night, and the nights get longer.
Money: Tools, shelter upgrades, cosmetics, supply drops.
Day 8-28: Camp progression, seasonal winters, survivor ranks.
Build: Medium. The closest to the proven formula in this lane, which is both the safety and the risk.

**20. Countdown City**
Loop: A city with a timer over it. Evacuate as many residents as you can before it hits zero. Every run, a different city.
Money: Vehicles, equipment, cosmetics, extra time.
Day 8-28: City roster, rescue rankings, weekly cities.
Build: Heavy. Cities are expensive. Strong idea, expensive execution.

## Lane 5 — Defend the Line
*Plants vs Brainrots hit 8.65M concurrent. The lane is proven and under-populated.*

**21. Lawn Order**
Loop: Pests are crossing your lawn. Place defenders. Your neighbour's lawn is connected to yours, so what gets past them arrives at your fence.
Money: Defender types, lawn expansions, fertiliser, cosmetics.
Day 8-28: Defender collection, lawn levels, neighbourhood rankings.
Build: Cheap. **The connected-lawn mechanic is the co-play twist nobody in this lane has done.**

**22. Sandcastle Siege**
Loop: Build a sandcastle. The tide comes in on a timer and the kids down the beach are throwing things.
Money: Tools, decorations, wall types, cosmetics.
Day 8-28: Castle blueprints, beach rankings, seasonal tides.
Build: Cheap. Summer evergreen with genuine charm.

**23. Anthill**
Loop: Defend the colony. Assign ants to roles, hold the tunnels, feed the queen.
Money: Ant types, tunnel expansions, queen upgrades, cosmetics.
Day 8-28: Colony progression, species collection, seasonal invasions.
Build: Medium. Underground layout is a nice change from the genre's flat plots.

**24. Snow Fort**
Loop: Build a fort, stock the snowballs, hold the hill against the other team.
Money: Fort pieces, snowball types, cosmetics, team banners.
Day 8-28: Fort blueprints, team ladders, winter seasons.
Build: Cheap. Naturally seasonal, which means a guaranteed December spike every year.

**25. Ward**
Loop: Place wards to hold back what comes out of the dark. Each ward you collect changes what the others do.
Money: Ward types, slots, reroll with disclosed odds, cosmetics.
Day 8-28: Ward collection with combinatorial depth, weekly incursions.
Build: Medium. The combination system is where the long tail lives.

## Lane 6 — Collect & Trade
*A vibrant trading economy is what keeps these games alive in year two.*

**26. Cryptid Cards**
Loop: Hunt cryptids in the field, photograph them, and the photograph becomes a tradeable card. The card market is the real game.
Money: Cameras, lenses, expedition passes, album pages.
Day 8-28: The market itself. Card rarity, completing sets, trading up.
Build: Medium. **Build the trade escrow properly or this dies of dupes in week three.**

**27. Fossil Dig**
Loop: Dig, extract, identify, trade. You do not know what you have until you clean it.
Money: Tools, dig site access, cleaning kits, display cases.
Day 8-28: A museum you fill, species index, trading for the pieces you lack.
Build: Cheap. The slow reveal of a fossil is inherently compelling.

**28. Flea Market**
Loop: Rent a stall, stock it from scavenging runs, and haggle with other players in real time.
Money: Stall sizes, storage, signage, cosmetics.
Day 8-28: Your stall and reputation persist. Regulars come back to you.
Build: Medium. Player-to-player pricing is the appeal and the exploit surface.

**29. Bottle Caps**
Loop: Scavenge a ruined world for bottle caps. Caps are currency, collectible, and the only thing anyone wants.
Money: Detectors, bags, safehouse slots, cosmetics.
Day 8-28: Cap varieties, safehouse building, trade circles.
Build: Cheap. One currency that is also the collection is elegant design.

**30. Sticker Album**
Loop: Earn packs, open them with fully disclosed odds, trade duplicates, complete pages.
Money: Packs, album expansions, trade slots, cosmetics.
Day 8-28: Page completion is a perfect long-horizon goal, and duplicates force interaction.
Build: Cheap. **Disclose every probability. This is the design most likely to draw regulatory attention if done carelessly.**

## Lane 7 — Round Games
*Short sessions, high replay. Murder Mystery 2 hit an all-time peak of 1,349,555 in August 2026, so the lane is very much alive.*

**31. Prop Heist**
Loop: One team hides as props. The other team hunts. But the props are also stealing things while hidden.
Money: Prop skins, hunter gear, cosmetics, round passes.
Day 8-28: Prop collection, ranked ladder, weekly maps.
Build: Cheap. Prop hunt is proven; the stealing layer is the new part.

**32. Chef Duel**
Loop: Two teams, one kitchen, a surprise ingredient and a countdown. Judged at the end.
Money: Kitchen tools, recipe books, chef cosmetics.
Day 8-28: Recipe collection, chef ranks, weekly ingredients.
Build: Medium. Cooking chaos produces outstanding clips.

**33. Tag Empire**
Loop: Tag someone and their territory becomes yours. The map is repainted continuously and the round ends when the clock does.
Money: Speed cosmetics, trails, taunt emotes, team banners.
Day 8-28: Ranked ladder, seasonal maps, team standings.
Build: Cheap. Tag is universally understood and needs no tutorial.

**34. Rising Floor**
Loop: The floor comes up. Build, climb, or be caught. Last one standing wins the round.
Money: Building blocks, cosmetics, emotes.
Day 8-28: Ranked ladder, block collection, weekly hazards.
Build: Cheap. Immediate, tense, and endlessly clippable.

**35. The Understudy**
Loop: A theatre troupe rehearsing a play. One cast member is not who they say they are. Find them before opening night.
Money: Costumes, stage cosmetics, role passes.
Day 8-28: Play collection, troupe ranks, weekly productions.
Build: Medium. Social deduction with a genuinely fresh setting.

## Lane 8 — Make & Show
*Creative and social. Dress to Impress proved that judging each other is a complete game.*

**36. Fit Check**
Loop: Dress to a theme, but you are scored as a crew of four, so your worst outfit drags everyone down.
Money: Clothing, accessories, dye, crew banners.
Day 8-28: Wardrobe collection, crew rankings, weekly themes.
Build: Medium. The crew scoring is the twist and it converts a solo game into a co-play one.

**37. Room Raters**
Loop: Decorate a room to a weekly theme. Other players tour it and rate it. Your home persists.
Money: Furniture, wallpapers, room expansions, lighting.
Day 8-28: A home you keep building, furniture collection, rating history.
Build: Cheap. Furniture is cheap content and players will grind for it indefinitely.

**38. Band Practice**
Loop: Four players, four instruments, one song. Play it together badly, then less badly.
Money: Instruments, venues, cosmetics, song packs.
Day 8-28: Your band persists as a crew. Setlists, venue progression, weekly songs.
Build: Heavy. Audio sync is genuinely difficult. Enormous upside if it works.

**39. Parade Float**
Loop: A team builds a parade float against a clock, then it rolls down the route and everyone watches.
Money: Float parts, decorations, themes, cosmetics.
Day 8-28: Part collection, parade rankings, seasonal parades.
Build: Medium. The reveal moment is a natural clip.

**40. Graffiti Alley**
Loop: Shared walls. Spray, layer, paint over each other. The alley persists between sessions.
Money: Spray cans, colours, stencils, wall space.
Day 8-28: The alley itself is the persistence. Galleries, weekly walls.
Build: Medium. Moderation is the hard problem and must be solved before launch.

## Lane 9 — The Grown-Up Lane
*Roblox pays 42% more DevEx for age-verified US adult spend, and the 18-34 cohort grew over 50% year on year while monetizing about 50% higher. Almost nobody is building for it.*

**41. Estate Sale**
Loop: Walk an estate, appraise what is there, bid against other players, and flip what you win.
Money: Appraisal tools, storage, shop upgrades, auction access.
Day 8-28: Your shop and reputation persist. Item knowledge compounds genuinely.
Build: Medium. Appeals to a demographic with money and nothing built for them.

**42. Dispatch**
Loop: You are emergency dispatch. Calls come in, you allocate units, and you cannot be everywhere.
Money: Shift passes, cosmetics, city unlocks.
Day 8-28: City progression, dispatcher ranks, weekly scenarios.
Build: Medium. Tense, text-light, adult, and cheap to build.

**43. Night Market**
Loop: Run a stall in an atmospheric night market. Source stock, set prices, read the crowd.
Money: Stall upgrades, stock access, decor, lighting.
Day 8-28: Reputation, regulars, seasonal markets.
Build: Medium. Mood is the product.

**44. Restoration**
Loop: Take something ruined and make it whole. Strip, repair, refinish, reveal.
Money: Tools, materials, workshop upgrades, project access.
Day 8-28: A workshop you build, a portfolio of finished pieces.
Build: Cheap. Restoration content is reliably popular and deeply satisfying.

**45. Signal**
Loop: A cold-war listening post. Tune, transcribe, decide what matters and report it.
Money: Equipment, station upgrades, cosmetics.
Day 8-28: A dossier that builds over weeks, station progression.
Build: Cheap. Atmospheric, adult, and almost entirely audio and UI.

## Lane 10 — Clip Machines
*Moments went fully live at RDC 2026 and share prompts carry an invite link. Tiny loops, built to be filmed.*

**46. Ragdoll Roulette**
Loop: Pick a door. One of them does something appalling to your ragdoll. Everyone watches.
Money: Ragdoll skins, emotes, door passes.
Day 8-28: Outcome collection, daily rotation, leaderboards.
Build: Cheap. Pure clip fuel.

**47. One Chance**
Loop: One attempt per day at something very hard. That is the whole game. Come back tomorrow.
Money: Cosmetics, extra attempts, skins.
Day 8-28: **The daily limit is itself the retention mechanic**, which is elegant and rare.
Build: Cheap. The scarcity does the work.

**48. Ten Second Tower**
Loop: Ten seconds to build the tallest tower you can. Then physics gets a vote.
Money: Block skins, tools, cosmetics.
Day 8-28: Daily and weekly leaderboards, block collection.
Build: Cheap. Perfect for the end-of-2026 browser instant-play link.

**49. Dare Ya**
Loop: The server votes on a dare. Someone does it. Points are awarded. Repeat.
Money: Cosmetics, dare packs, emotes.
Day 8-28: Reputation, dare collection, weekly dares.
Build: Cheap. Moderation needs real thought before launch.

**50. Yeet Club**
Loop: Launch yourself as far as possible. Upgrade the launcher. Launch further.
Money: Launchers, cosmetics, trails, upgrade packs.
Day 8-28: Distance leaderboards, launcher collection, weekly maps.
Build: Cheap. The most derivative idea here, and the most reliably profitable per hour of work.

## How to actually choose

Do not pick by which sounds most fun to build. Pick by these three, in order:

1. **Can you commit to a weekly slot for six months?** If not, choose from Lane 10, where the loop is small enough to survive light operation. Everything else dies without live operations.
2. **Do you have an artist?** Lanes 3, 5 and 8 live or die on art direction. Lanes 1, 2 and 10 survive on charm and physics.
3. **What is your honest build capacity?** Cheap means a two-person eight-week build. Six of the fifty are marked Heavy and none of those should be a first project.

Then prototype the first sixty seconds and nothing else. If the first minute is not fun with placeholder cubes, no amount of content will save it. That test costs a weekend and has killed more bad projects than any amount of planning.
