# Better Ideas
### Fewer, stronger, explained plainly

The first fifty had filler in it. This is the sharper set. Each one is explained the way you would explain it to a friend in ten seconds, because if it cannot survive that, it cannot survive a game tile either.

Ranked best first. Build cost is **Cheap** for two people in eight weeks, **Medium** for twelve, **Heavy** for a real team.

---

## 1. LANTERN

**What it is:** It is dark. Your lamp is slowly dying. Only another player can relight it.

**The clever bit:** You physically cannot survive alone. Not "it is easier with friends" — alone, your light goes out and the dark takes you. Every other player is a walking match. Strangers save each other within thirty seconds of meeting, and that is the entire emotional engine.

**Why you come back:** The world is enormous and unmapped, and the map you fill in persists. Deeper regions need bigger groups. Your lantern upgrades permanently.

**What you sell:** Lantern designs, flame colours, longer burn time, beacons that mark the map for everyone, cosmetics that make you visible in the dark and therefore findable.

**Build:** Cheap. Darkness is the cheapest art direction that exists — you light a small radius and the rest is black. This is the single best cost-to-beauty ratio available.

**Why I rank it first:** Roblox's algorithm now rewards intentional co-play as a headline signal, and no game I found makes cooperation *mechanically mandatory* rather than merely encouraged. Lantern does it with one rule a five-year-old understands.

## 2. SKYTREE

**What it is:** You and your friends grow one enormous tree together over two real-world weeks, and what it turns into depends on what you all fed it.

**The clever bit:** The two-week timer *is* the retention system. It is not a weekly league bolted onto a loop. You come back on day eight because that is when your tree is nearly grown, and you cannot pay to rush it.

**Why you come back:** Your tree matures on a schedule you do not control. Rare species only happen through cross-pollination with another group, so you need other people's trees too.

**What you sell:** Seeds, tools, grove decorations, nutrients that shape the species rather than the speed.

**Build:** Cheap.

**Note:** This is the idea currently in front of the council. Their verdict is in `council/`.

---

## 3. DIG DOWN

**What it is:** Everyone on the server digs the same hole. It never resets. The deeper it gets, the stranger things get down there.

**The clever bit:** The hole is permanent and collective. You are not digging *a* hole, you are digging *the* hole, and the depth marker on the wall has other players' names on it. Someone dug past 4,000 metres last Tuesday and you can go stand where they stopped.

**Why you come back:** Depth is a number that only goes up, and it goes up faster when more people dig. New layers unlock at depths nobody has reached yet, so the frontier is a genuine community event.

**What you sell:** Drills, lights, carry capacity, elevator checkpoints, cosmetics visible on the leaderboard.

**Build:** Cheap. A vertical procedural shaft is about the cheapest infinite content there is.

**The risk:** Mining games are a crowded lane on Roblox. The shared permanent hole is the differentiator and it has to be loud in the marketing or this reads as another mining simulator.

---

## 4. ROPE

**What it is:** You are tied to a stranger. Good luck.

**The clever bit:** That is the whole game. Two players, one rope, physics, and an obstacle course. Everything funny about it emerges from the rope without you designing any of it.

**Why you come back:** New courses weekly, a partner rating system, and the fact that it is different every time because the other end of the rope is a different person.

**What you sell:** Rope skins, character cosmetics, emotes, course packs.

**Build:** Cheap. Genuinely a two-week prototype.

**Why it is here:** Of everything on this list, this is the most likely to produce a clip that travels, and the research is unambiguous that YouTube and short-form video were the cold start for every recent hit. It is also the least defensible against cloning, so it wins on speed or not at all.

---

## 5. FEED THE BEAST

**What it is:** Something enormous lives under your town. Feed it every day. Do not let it get hungry.

**The clever bit:** It is Skytree with teeth. You get the same custodianship and the same real-world timer, but with dread instead of serenity, which gives you the drama the gentle version lacks. If the town neglects it, it surfaces, and everyone loses something real.

**Why you come back:** The hunger meter does not pause when you log off. Your town needs you specifically, and the consequence of everyone shrugging is visible and shared.

**What you sell:** Food types, town buildings, defences, cosmetics, offering decorations.

**Build:** Medium. The beast needs to be genuinely impressive when it surfaces, and that is the one expensive asset.

---

## 6. THE SHOP

**What it is:** You run a little shop. Real players are your customers. You set your own prices.

**The clever bit:** Your reputation persists and is visible. Overcharge and people stop coming. Be generous to a new player and they come back for a year. It is a game about being known, which almost nothing on the platform offers a child.

**Why you come back:** Regulars. Genuinely — people return to the shopkeeper who was nice to them, and shopkeepers return to serve them.

**What you sell:** Shop sizes, decor, signage, storage, stock access.

**Build:** Medium. Player-set pricing is the appeal and also the exploit surface; it needs care.

---

## 7. THE EGG

**What it is:** One egg. Keep it warm for a week. What hatches depends on how you looked after it.

**The clever bit:** The payoff moment is enormous and completely unspoiled — nobody knows what is inside until it opens, including you. That hatch is the most clippable single event on this entire list.

**Why you come back:** A one-week timer you cannot buy your way past, and the care you put in during days one to six determines day seven.

**What you sell:** Warmth sources, nests, shelter upgrades, cosmetics for whatever hatches.

**Build:** Cheap.

---

## 8. LIGHTHOUSE

**What it is:** Two to four of you run a lighthouse overnight. Write down what you see in the fog. Some of it should not be written down.

**The clever bit:** The co-op anomaly lane won every award at RDC 2026 with *Animal Hospital*, exceeded 700,000 concurrent players, and still has exactly one dominant title. That is a proven, award-winning, under-populated lane.

**Why you come back:** A logbook that fills over weeks, escalating nights, keeper ranks.

**What you sell:** Lamp upgrades, equipment, cosmetics, shift passes.

**Build:** Medium. One building, heavy atmosphere, tiny asset list.

---

## 9. LAWN ORDER

**What it is:** Pests are crossing your lawn. Put things down to stop them. Whatever gets past your neighbour arrives at your fence next.

**The clever bit:** The lawns are connected in a chain. Your neighbour's competence is your problem. That converts a solo tower-defense game into a cooperative one without changing a single tower.

**Why you come back:** Defender collection, lawn levels, neighbourhood rankings.

**What you sell:** Defender types, lawn expansions, fertiliser, cosmetics.

**Build:** Cheap. *Plants vs Brainrots* proved this lane at 8.65 million concurrent players.

---

## 10. HAND-ME-DOWN

**What it is:** When you start over, everything you owned is given to a brand-new player. You get a note back from them.

**The clever bit:** This is less a game than the best mechanic on this page, and it can be bolted onto almost any of the others. It turns the rebirth reset — normally a pure loss — into an act of generosity with a face attached. New players get a windfall from a stranger and immediately want to pay it forward.

**Why you come back:** To see who got your things and what they did with them.

**What you sell:** Whatever the host game sells. Plus the gift wrapping, honestly.

**Build:** Cheap as a layer. Do not build it standalone.

---

## 11. SNATCH A STOMPER

**What it is:** Two teams of four. Things with legs make you money. The other team can carry them out of your warehouse.

**The clever bit:** The steal formula, rebuilt so being robbed is a team event with a rescue in it rather than a solo humiliation you quit over.

**Why you come back:** Persistent crews, weekly league, collection, trading.

**What you sell:** Multipliers, auto-collect, crew perks, server-wide luck boosts.

**Build:** Cheap. Full design in `01-GAME-DESIGN.md`.

---

## 12. WEATHERMAN

**What it is:** One player controls the weather. Everyone else has to survive it. Then you swap.

**The clever bit:** The power rotates, so everyone gets to be the villain and everyone gets to be the victim, and nobody stays mad. Asymmetric power that takes turns is an old, reliable, under-used design.

**Why you come back:** Weather collection, ranked play, weekly maps.

**What you sell:** Weather types, survivor gear, cosmetics, emotes.

**Build:** Medium. Weather effects are the cost.

---

## 13. BRIDGE

**What it is:** There is a chasm. The whole server builds one bridge across it. It takes days.

**The clever bit:** A single shared construction that persists between sessions, with every plank traceable to whoever laid it. When it finally connects, everyone crosses together, and that is a genuine event.

**Why you come back:** It is not finished yet, and it will not finish without you.

**What you sell:** Materials, tools, decorative plank styles, lanterns along the span.

**Build:** Cheap.

---

## 14. CRYPTID CARDS

**What it is:** Go into the woods, photograph something that should not exist, and the photo becomes a card you can trade.

**The clever bit:** The trading market is the real game, and the cards are player-made, so every card has a photographer's name on it. Rarity is partly about the creature and partly about who shot it.

**Why you come back:** Completing sets, the market, and the fact that rare creatures appear on a schedule.

**What you sell:** Cameras, lenses, film, album pages, expedition access.

**Build:** Medium. **Build the trade escrow properly or dupes kill it in week three.**

---

## 15. ONE CHANCE

**What it is:** One attempt per day at something very hard. That is the entire game. Come back tomorrow.

**The clever bit:** The scarcity is the product. It is the Wordle trick — a tiny loop that owns a two-minute slot in your day and generates conversation precisely because everyone got the same one attempt.

**Why you come back:** Because you get one more tomorrow, and everyone is comparing.

**What you sell:** Cosmetics, skins, trails. Selling extra attempts destroys the design, exactly as selling time-skips would destroy Skytree.

**Build:** Cheap.

## What changed from the first fifty

Three things, and they are worth stating because they are the actual lesson:

**The best ideas make cooperation mandatory rather than optional.** Lantern, Skytree, Dig Down, Bridge and Feed the Beast all break if you play alone. That is not a coincidence — it is the design response to Roblox making intentional co-play a headline ranking signal in June 2026.

**The best retention mechanic is a clock you cannot buy.** Skytree, The Egg and One Chance all refuse to sell the wait. That is painful for monetization and it is precisely why it works, because it makes the return visit non-negotiable rather than purchasable.

**The best hooks are one sentence with a twist in it.** "You are tied to a stranger." "Only another player can relight your lamp." "Everyone digs the same hole." If the hook needs a second sentence, the tile will not convert.
