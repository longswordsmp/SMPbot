# The Believer's Case for SKYTREE

*Council position paper. Written against `00-BRIEF.md` and appendices C, D and E. Speculation is labelled.*

---

## 1. The core thesis

Every incumbent retains through **greed and fear of loss** — you come back because someone might take your thing. Skytree retains through **obligation to specific named people**, and nobody in this genre has built on that. The mercenary point: a return visit produced by obligation is *indistinguishable to the ranker* from one produced by fun, and far cheaper to manufacture. A child who forgets your game comes back when reminded; a child who told nineteen people he is on blight duty comes back because they will notice he didn't. Tamagotchi shipped ninety million units on nothing but a timer and a dependent that visibly deteriorated — no content, no progression, no social layer. Neopets ran that hook for two decades; Farmville turned "your neighbour needs you" into the best-performing viral mechanic in social gaming history. Skytree puts that custodial hook inside the Roblox formula genre at exactly the moment Roblox began paying, in distribution, for the Day 8-28 return and for "intentional co-play" (June 15, 2026). The lane is empty not because it is bad but because incumbents cannot enter it: appendix C's own reading is that "studios treat PvP hostility as the retention engine," and Grow a Garden 2 *added* stealing in June 2026. Turning hostility off would cannibalise the loop printing their money. That is an innovator's dilemma, and it is the whole opportunity.

## 2. Who actually plays this

The acquisition unit is not a player, **it is a Grove**. You acquire one organiser and get up to twenty installs. That changes every growth assumption below.

**Maya, 13 — the Grove Keeper.** Runs the group chat; already organises the sleepover and the Blox Fruits raid. Session: 25-40 minutes after school — reads overnight growth, assigns blight duty, screenshots the canopy into the Grove's Community feed, negotiates pollen with a rival Grove. She leaves Steal a Brainrot because it has no job for her: it makes her a victim with no way to lead. Skytree gives her a raid leader's role with no raid. **Discovery:** a mid-tier YouTube video ("MY GROVE GREW A CLOCK TREE") — the cold start that took Dead Rails from hundreds of CCU to 100K+ after one Flamingo video. She then recruits, which is the real growth engine.

**Leo, 9 — the Checker.** Motivation is shallow and pure: *is it bigger than yesterday?* Session: 3-6 minutes, twice daily, on a phone (72-80% of platform activity is mobile). Drops nutrients, squashes two beetles, leaves. Not a new behaviour — Grow a Garden trained millions of children to check on a thing they cannot rush, and he is leaving it because his garden plateaued, not because he stopped wanting the loop. **Discovery:** Maya's invite, or a Roblox Moments clip (Moments went fully live in the US at RDC 2026; Experience Page videos entered the feed in late September 2026).

**Ren, 15 — the Collector/Broker.** Motivation: the species index and its combinatorics — which feed ratios make which tree, which cross-pollinations unlock what. Session: 45-90 minutes plus hours in Discord and on a fan wiki he is building. He already maintains a Steal a Brainrot value list; appendix C notes third-party sites cover these economies "like stock tickers." Ren is a free content engine and the game's SEO layer. **Discovery:** himself, in a Discord trading channel.

**Dani, 19 — the Co-Player.** Plays with an 8-year-old sibling. Motivation: something they can do together where neither ruins the other's afternoon — the appetite Animal Hospital proved at 700K+ concurrent with a 94-95% rating from 1.1M votes. She is worth ~42% more per dollar: age-verified US adult spend in R15 games DevExes at $0.0054 against $0.0038. **Discovery:** how she found Animal Hospital — a friend, or the Home friends-activity row once two friends are in a Grove.

## 3. Who spends money, and why

Concede the hard part first: **the design gives up the whale.** No 7,499-Robux admin pass. ARPPU will sit below Steal a Brainrot's. That is a real cost, deliberately paid.

- **Feeds and fertilisers, 49-199 R$** — not a time-skip but a *steering* purchase: Ashfeed nudges the species table toward Storm Tree. You buy influence over a collective outcome, which means you buy it in order to argue for it. Appendix D identifies the 25-49 R$ short boost as "the highest-converting type" in the genre: same shape, same frequency, same low deliberation, with the time-skip surgically removed.
- **Weather calls, 199-375 R$** — server-wide, visible, attributed to the buyer by name. Not a gamepass, a Twitch bit: the purchase *is* the performance, the highest social visibility per Robux on the board.
- **Branch plots, 199-399 R$** — permanent personal space on the shared tree; a cosmetic nineteen specific people see daily for two weeks. Roblox's UGC avatar economy proves children pay for visible identity, and almost none of that spend is currently addressable inside a formula game.
- **Grove goods, 499-999 R$** — banner, nameplate, twentieth slot, canopy treatment. Bought by Maya, the highest-intent spender in the game because she carries social responsibility for the group. This is the Pls Donate impulse — paying to be seen giving — relocated *inside* one experience, which matters because cross-experience pass and dev-product sales were disabled on May 29/30, 2026. In-experience is now the only surviving venue for it.
- **Seasonal cosmetic pass, 749 R$**, matching Grow a Garden's premium track; two arcs per month-long season is a natural beat.

**The social-spending argument, properly.** In a solo-progression game a purchase is private, so it competes only against the buyer's own desire — which is why those games need whales. Around one shared object watched by twenty people, a purchase is seen, named and reciprocated. That converts people who would never buy a multiplier (Pls Donate is the proof that public giving converts non-payers) and raises repeat frequency among payers, because the last one got noticed. It also caps the top end. That is the trade.

**Why the parent's card comes out** *(speculative, but mechanistic)*. The ask changes shape. "I need 400 Robux for the ban hammer" gets declined. "Our grove is growing a Glass Tree and I want my own branch on it before Saturday" has a named group, a deadline and a non-violent object. Same money, materially different conversion with the person holding the card. With a 182-case federal MDL running and six state AGs suing, an ask that survives parental scrutiny is a business asset, not a softness.

## 4. Why the timing is right

1. **Ten-to-fourteen-day maturity → the Day 8-28 bucket.** The June 2026 ranker scores D1, D2-7 and D8-28 separately, and creators' loudest April 2026 complaint was that a brand-new game *cannot prove* D8-28. Skytree proves it on its first cohort, fourteen days after launch, because the payoff is mechanically located there. No other new game has a structural reason for a day-12 visit; they have a weekly league bolted on.
2. **Solo growth too slow → co-play at 100%, not at a ratio.** "Intentional co-play" is a headline signal. Incumbents generate co-play incidentally; here every qualified session is one by construction.
3. **No time-skip → the August 20, 2026 dual test is free money.** That test rewards games strong on retention *and* monetisation together. The non-obvious point: on almost every game those axes fight, because the timer-skip is the purchase that destroys the return visit it monetises. Every Skytree product is retention-neutral or retention-*positive* — a reason to show up, never a reason to skip showing up. This may be one of the few designs on Roblox where the two signals do not trade off.
4. **Cross-pollination → the "direct growth" signal.** Rare species need pollen from another Grove that grew something unusual, making recruitment and inter-group contact instrumentally necessary: Farmville's neighbour requirement with a collectible payoff instead of a nag.
5. **No media feed, autoplay or watch-to-earn → clean in the Kids and Select tiers.** After the late-August 2026 policy that delisted Steal An Egg, tier eligibility is a distribution advantage the incumbent formula is losing.
6. **Three-minute check-ins → the best shape for RDC 2026's standalone Android apps and end-of-2026 Chrome play.** "Check my tree in a browser tab" ports. A steal-and-defend tycoon does not.

## 5. The upside case

Benchmarks: DAU ≈ 16-32× average CCU; $10,000-70,000 net per month per 1,000 average CCU.

**A second non-obvious arithmetic point.** Payer conversion is a percentage of *DAU*, not CCU. A game with 4-minute sessions has a far higher DAU:CCU ratio than one with 90-minute sessions — the top of the band or above. At equal conversion, the short-session game therefore monetises *better per concurrent player*. The objection "sessions are too short" is arithmetically an advantage, and it partly offsets the lower Robux-per-visit that giving up the time-skip costs.

**Realistic good outcome:** 5,000 average CCU sustained after month three, roughly 100,000-160,000 DAU. Discount the rule of thumb honestly for a low-extraction design — 1.5-3 gross R$/visit against the genre's 2-6, and 1.5-2.5% payer conversion — and it lands near **$15,000-60,000 per month net**: a real two-person business, above the strategy brief's realistic-good bar of "five figures of concurrent players and a few thousand dollars a month."

**Tail outcome:** Animal Hospital's shape — 700K+ peak, 2.27B visits in sixteen months, four Innovation Awards. At 100,000-200,000 sustained average CCU the rule of thumb implies **$1M-14M per month**. Flagged honestly as the one-in-several-thousand case; the 25M-concurrent outcome is available to nobody in 2026, since two of its three conditions (a spike-rewarding ranker, a studio with an audience to cross-promote from) no longer hold. The tail worth underwriting is non-revenue: dozens of owned original species. Steal a Brainrot has a PhatMojo plush line and a Story Kitchen film deal only for characters it controls — which is why it is in court trying to invalidate Mementum's copyright.

## 6. Why the obvious objections are wrong

**"Two weeks is too long for a nine-year-old."** Right about the nine-year-old, wrong about the design. Leo holds a twenty-four-hour intention and is asked for nothing more: *the tree holds the long intention, the Grove holds Leo.* What two weeks buys is payoff scarcity — a species reveal twenty-six times a year rather than continuously is an event worth filming. Wordle had a 24-hour timer and no content between plays and became one of the most-shared games ever, because a shared clock is what makes conversation possible. A game everyone can play any amount at any time generates no conversation; a Grove all on day nine of the same tree generates daily synchronised talk. The genre half-knows this — hence every incumbent's fixed Saturday slot and the third-party sites that exist purely to track admin-abuse times. Skytree makes that ritual endogenous rather than bolted on.

**"Nothing is stolen, so there's no drama."** Partly right, and the concession is real: betrayal clips are this genre's YouTube fuel and giving them up raises acquisition cost. But drama is relocated, not removed. Blight is a raid boss on a clock, and a Grove that loses a twelve-day tree on day twelve is a *better* video than a steal, because the audience has twelve days of context. Stronger still, the sharpest conflict is internal: twenty people with divergent preferences decide what the tree becomes, and one member's feeding can spoil a rare species. That is Twitch Plays Pokémon, which produced more content, lore and factional warfare than any betrayal loop in history out of a game with no PvP and one mechanic — a group failing to coordinate. Cross-pollination then manufactures inter-Grove rivalry without manufacturing theft.

**"Waiting is boring."** True of an empty wait, so don't build one. Every session is active — gather, carry, feed, fight blight, prune — it simply doesn't advance the clock, and idle games have shipped that structure to hundreds of millions. Critically, the species outcome is *determined during the wait*, so each of the fourteen days contains a decision. The wait is not downtime, it is the deliberation period.

**"Monetisation is weak."** Concede ARPPU, then three answers. (a) It compensates on payer count and frequency: cheap, social, repeatable products with the DAU:CCU arithmetic in their favour. (b) The whale line forgone is precisely the under-13 line Roblox's Q2 2026 call blamed for the collapse — "monetization per hour was below forecast, particularly for the under-13 cohort" — and that the MDL is about. Designing around a revenue line that is simultaneously collapsing and under federal litigation is not weakness. (c) Under the August 2026 dual signal, non-cannibalising monetisation is worth more in *distribution* than the same revenue extracted destructively.

**"No proven comparable."** Factually wrong. Animal Hospital is the comparable for social structure — mandatory co-op, legible jobs — and in 2026 it took Best New Game, People's Choice, Best Innovation in Creative Direction and the Builderman Award while *no "Steal a" or "Grow a" title won anything*. Grow a Garden is the comparable for economics and the mesh-and-texture content pipeline; Tamagotchi, Neopets and Farmville for the custodial timer. What lacks a comparable is the combination — and the test for whether an uncombined idea is empty or merely unoccupied is whether an incumbent *could* occupy it. Here they cannot, without killing the hostility loop that is their retention engine.

**"Fourteen clones in forty-eight hours."** Real and unavoidable. But note what a Skytree clone inherits: an empty Grove. The clonable asset is code; the retaining asset is a twenty-person social graph that took two weeks to form. A "Steal a" clone is playable by a stranger in thirty seconds, which is exactly why cloning works there. This design is unusually clone-resistant for structural reasons — rare in this genre, and worth pricing in.

## 7. What would have to be true

1. **That a Grove can be filled at low population.** The retention engine needs a populated social unit, and 300 concurrent players cannot form twenty-person Groves. Grove size must scale with population — four to six at launch, twenty later — designed in, not patched. This is the most likely way the design dies.
2. **That obligation converts to return visits at 2026 Roblox rates.** My Tamagotchi/Neopets/Farmville evidence is from other eras and platforms with different norms; current Roblox play is transactional. This is the load-bearing speculation in my case and I will not pretend otherwise.
3. **That cheap, social, cosmetic and steering purchases reach 1.5-2.5% payer conversion.** Unproven for this product shape; all the conversion data in appendix D comes from games that sell power.
4. **That "intentional co-play" carries real weight in the June 2026 ranker.** Roblox called it a headline signal, but the published per-signal weights could not be retrieved in the research. If the weight is small, my timing argument degrades to "a nice co-op game in a contracting market."
5. **That two people can hold a fixed weekly ritual for six months** and staff twenty-six scheduled species reveals a year. Appendix E is blunt that missing week three is how these games die.

If one through four hold, this is the best-shaped product for the 2026 algorithm anyone on this council is likely to see. If two fails, it is a pleasant game nobody comes back to.
