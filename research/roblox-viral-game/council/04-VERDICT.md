# The Verdict
### Council seat 4 of 4. Final.

---

## 1. The verdict

**Do not build Skytree. Build LANTERN, starting Monday.** The Believer identified the right asset — mandatory cooperation, owned IP, a design native to the June 2026 ranker — then welded it to a fourteen-day uncontrollable clock and a twenty-person cross-server Grove that carry every one of the Skeptic's surviving objections. Those two things are separable, and once separated the choice is obvious: keep the cooperation, kill the clock. Lantern is the same thesis with cooperation happening in thirty seconds between strangers in one server instead of over fourteen days between twenty named children, and that single change retires the compound-churn kill shot, the clip-supply problem, the Grove-versus-server contradiction, the monetization handicap, the moderation exposure, and — the point nobody made — the fact that Skytree cannot be cheaply tested at all. The Investor's case rests on "60% of the risk retired for $300 and three weeks." That is false for Skytree, whose test loop is longer than its build. It is true for Lantern, whose core claim can be falsified in one evening.

---

## 2. Who won, and on what

**The fourteen-day timer: retention or churn?** **Skeptic, decisively.** His sentence "the tree will be there on day 8 regardless" is the design's confession, and the Believer never answers it. Worse, the Believer's own citation refutes him: Wordle is evidence for a *twenty-four-hour* clock, and Tamagotchi punishes absence within *hours*. Both proofs he offers for a long clock are proofs of a short one. A timer that runs without you is a timer you can safely ignore.

**Is "obligation retention" real?** **Split, and the split is the whole verdict.** The Believer is right that a return produced by obligation is indistinguishable to the ranker from one produced by fun — that is the sharpest line in his paper. The Skeptic is right that obligation to nineteen people over a fortnight is a quit trigger, because the cheapest way a child discharges social debt is to leave the group. But note what the Skeptic actually killed: *obligation at that scale and duration*. Obligation to one stranger for three seconds is not a burden at all — it is a gift. The Believer's mechanism survives; his implementation does not.

**Compound Grove churn.** **Skeptic, outright. The kill shot lands and the Believer's answer is circular.** "Scale Grove size with population" addresses the wrong variable. The problem is not how many players exist, it is cohort decay across the tree's lifetime. At the corpus's own 25% D1, a Grove of four has roughly one active member by day four — shrinking the Grove makes variance *worse*, because one churn is now 25% of your roster. The only real fix is backfilling Groves with new players, which restores exactly the anonymous shock absorber that the persistent named roster was invented to remove. The fix destroys the thesis.

**The clip problem.** **Skeptic wins, but his reasoning is half wrong.** The spoilage claim — that a video of the Glass Tree hands you the reward in nine seconds — is false, and this council's own idea list proves it: entry 7, THE EGG, has an identical reveal structure and the author calls it "the most clippable single event on this list." Children watch egg-opening and mutation videos endlessly and still buy eggs, because in a collection game the payoff is *possession*, not *information*. But the real objection is supply rate, and it is unanswerable: one clippable moment per Grove per fortnight, and that moment only has stakes for someone who watched the preceding fourteen days. A cold viewer sees a mesh change. No creator can build a series on it. Since YouTube was the cold start for every recent hit, a design with near-zero clip supply and mandatory prior context is undiscoverable in 2026. Fatal on its own.

**The monetization ladder above 749 R$.** **Investor, over both.** The Skeptic overstates — "removes most of the revenue" is wrong; the correct statement is the Investor's, that the refusal costs ~two-thirds of revenue per player and therefore requires ~3× the CCU for the same money. A price, not a veto. And there *is* one product above 1,500 R$ that sells neither time, nor power, nor randomness: **permanent named visibility** — being known, which is what Pls Donate monetizes. The Skeptic missed it. But it needs a world that persists and is seen by *strangers*, which a private twenty-person Grove is not. The answer exists; Skytree cannot reach it. Lantern can.

**Grove versus server, and the silent scope escalation.** **Skeptic, and this is the most underrated point in the entire council — nobody rebutted it.** `03-FIFTY-IDEAS.md` specified eight players, one server, per-server tree, rated **Cheap**. The brief silently became twenty players, cross-server, persistent, with MessagingService sync and contended DataStore writes. For two people in eight weeks that is not a scope increase, it is a different project. And the trap closes twice: Roblox can only measure co-play it can observe, which means same-server co-presence, so the cross-server Grove costs more to build *and* scores worse on the signal that justified it. The escalation destroyed the one property that made Skytree fundable.

**The Grow a Garden 2 natural experiment.** **Believer, on the narrow point.** This is the Skeptic's weakest argument. A game that peaks at 22.3M concurrent and settles at 63K thirteen months later has not lost an A/B test, it has completed a hype cycle — and its decay is fully confounded by the June 2026 discovery rewrite and the platform's own contraction. That GaG2 added stealing tells you what the studio believes monetizes, not what retains. The Believer wins this exchange. It does not save him, because it was never load-bearing.

**Empty lane or graveyard?** **Both, and the line between them is the decision.** For the fourteen-day shared clock: graveyard. The Skeptic is right that this is the Farmville and clan-timer shape every developer has known for fifteen years; it was tried at scale, and where it survives on mobile it survives on whale monetization Skytree refuses. For mandatory cooperation as such: genuinely empty, and the Believer's innovator's-dilemma test is the right test — incumbents cannot turn hostility off without cannibalising their own retention engine. Animal Hospital at 700K+ concurrent and four RDC awards is the live proof. The lane is real; the vehicle is wrong.

## 3. What everyone missed

**3.1 Skytree cannot be cheaply tested, and the Investor's own plan concedes it without noticing.** His Stage 1 runs the clock "compressed 24:1" and his gate G1.1 is "6 of 10 return at the *simulated* day 8." He proposes to validate a fourteen-day design by never once running it at fourteen days. Whatever that measures, it is not the product. To test D14 you need fourteen days plus recruitment — his Stage 2, eleven person-weeks. The asymmetry that is his entire investment case ("60% of uncertainty for $300 and three weeks") does not exist for this design. **A game whose test loop is longer than its build loop cannot be de-risked by staging.** Lantern's full emotional loop — meet a stranger in the dark, get relit — runs in thirty seconds, so it can be falsified on a Saturday evening with twelve friends.

**3.2 The Skeptic's 72-hour cycle and the Investor's 24:1 compression are the same recommendation, reached independently by two people who did not notice they had both voted to ship the compressed version.** Two of three councillors are already not backing the stated design. Read that as a result, not a coincidence.

**3.3 Skytree bundles two innovations that have no reason to ship together.** Mandatory cooperation is valuable, algorithm-favoured and clone-resistant. The multi-day uncontrollable clock is unproven, monetization-hostile, discovery-hostile and untestable. Bundled, a failure cannot be attributed to either. Unbundled, you ship the good one first.

**3.4 The adult-lane finding changes the game, not the audience — and it picks Lantern.** The Investor's Option C has the highest EV on his own board ($104,095, with only 41% in the tail) and he called it "the robust call," then recommended aging Skytree's audience while keeping Skytree. That does not work: a seven-year-old's game with an age gate is still a seven-year-old's game. What actually reads adult on Roblox is atmosphere and dread — darkness, scarcity, mutual rescue. **The Investor's highest-EV option and the author's top-ranked idea are the same product, and neither of them spotted it because Lantern was not on the table.**

**3.5 Honest concession against my own ruling.** The Believer's clone-resistance argument is correct and it favours Skytree: a Skytree clone inherits an empty Grove, while a Lantern clone is playable by a stranger in thirty seconds. Lantern's moat is speed and art direction, not a social graph. Accept that and move fast.

## 4. The decision

**Build LANTERN. One server. No Groves. No fourteen-day anything.**

- **Server-local, 12–20 players, one persistent map.** Everything in the server. This deletes objection 5.4 entirely and returns the build to genuinely Cheap.
- **Lamp burns down in 6–8 minutes.** Relight requires standing within a few studs of another player for ~3 seconds and costs the giver ~25% of their own fuel. Cooperation is mandatory *and* has a real price, so it is a decision, not a formality.
- **Death drops your unbanked map fragments where you fell.** Loss without theft. Another player can carry them back to you — a rescue clip, not a betrayal clip.
- **Beacons are the shared persistent object Skytree wanted, at a scale that survives churn.** A beacon takes one player one minute, lights a region permanently, and carries the placer's name. Nineteen people quitting does not stunt a beacon.
- **Region gating by lamp count:** region 2 needs 2 lit lamps present, region 4 needs 4. Mandatory co-op enforced by physics, not rules, and it scales with population automatically.
- **Map persistence per account** is the collection and the Day 8–28 hook — progress you cannot rush, moved from a clock you do not control to a world you explore.
- **R15-only from line one** (the Investor's G0.1 — free today, impossible after launch).
- **Monetization — and the purity handicap dies with the clock:** lantern skins 49–199, flame colours 99–249, permanent +25% burn time 399–599, season pass 749, and the answer to the Skeptic's challenge: **a named permanent beacon at 1,499 R$**, limited per region, seen forever by every stranger who passes it. It sells neither time, nor power over others, nor randomness. It sells being known.

**Designated fallback if Lantern fails week 1: DIG DOWN.** It is Skytree with the fatal parts removed — the same permanent shared object, no clock, churn-proof by construction (a hole does not care that nineteen people left, it is merely less deep), and high clip supply. It is already on the list and already rated Cheap. **Do not build the Skeptic's 72-hour Skytree.** It is a strictly worse Lantern: still needs Groves, still needs dozens of species meshes, still supply-starved for clips, and by his own estimate it only shrinks 80% of his objections to "manageable."

## 6. The kill criteria

- Tile test: **<8 of 10** correctly describe the game after 2 seconds → the hook failed. Stop.
- Private test: **<70%** of first stranger encounters produce an unprompted relight within 15 seconds → the rule does not self-teach. One redesign attempt, then stop.
- Public, on ≥300 players: **D1 < 25%** → stop. (The corpus benchmark is 25%; below it you are worse than the platform baseline on the metric the ranker scores first.)
- **Median session < 6 minutes** → stop.
- **<60% of sessions contain a relight given or received** → co-play is aspirational, not real, and the algorithm measures the real one. Stop.
- Five clip attempts, **zero watchable** → do not spend a dollar on YouTube. Stop.
- Week 6, on ≥500 players: **D7 < 12%** or **payer conversion < 1.0% of DAU** → stop. (The Investor's G2.2 and G2.4, adopted unchanged.)
- Week 10, 30 days after the first paid video: **avg CCU < 150**, or declining three consecutive weeks → stop.
- Standing rule: **no YouTuber spend until D7 ≥ 12% is measured.** Not one dollar, no exceptions, no "one more update."

## 7. The one thing that matters most

Cooperation is the asset and the fourteen-day clock is the liability — so ship the version of this idea you can prove wrong in a weekend, not the one that takes a fortnight to find out.
